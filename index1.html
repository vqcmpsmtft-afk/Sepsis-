ALGORITHM 3: EARLY-ONSET SEPSIS (EOS) PATHWAY

Step 1: Risk Assessment

MATERNAL/PERINATAL RISK FACTORS PRESENT?

  • Maternal fever ≥38°C during labor
  • Prolonged ROM ≥18 hours
  • Maternal GBS positive/unknown
  • Chorioamnionitis (clinical/histological)
  • Maternal UTI
  • Preterm birth <37 weeks
  • Inadequate intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis
↓

Step 2: Clinical Assessment

CLINICAL SIGNS OF SEPSIS

RESPIRATORY:

  • Tachypnea >60/min
  • Apnea
  • Grunting
  • Retractions
  • Cyanosis

CARDIOVASCULAR:

  • Tachycardia >160/min
  • Bradycardia <100/min
  • Capillary refill >3 sec
  • Hypotension

GENERAL:

  • Temperature instability (fever >38°C or hypothermia <36°C)
  • Lethargy/irritability
  • Poor feeding
  • Hypotonia
  • Seizures

GASTROINTESTINAL:

  • Vomiting
  • Abdominal distension
  • Jaundice (early onset)

SKIN:

  • Petechiae/purpura
  • Mottling
  • Pallor
↓

Step 3: Decision Pathway

Clinical Scenario Action Required
HIGH RISK:
Clinical signs + Risk factors		 FULL SEPSIS WORKUP + START ANTIBIOTICS IMMEDIATELY
MODERATE RISK:
Clinical signs OR multiple risk factors		 FULL SEPSIS WORKUP + START ANTIBIOTICS
LOW RISK:
Single minor risk factor, no clinical signs		 Limited workup (CBC, CRP)
Observe closely for 48 hours
Consider antibiotics based on results
↓

Step 4: Investigations for EOS

FULL SEPSIS WORKUP:

  • Blood culture (before antibiotics)
  • Complete blood count (CBC) with differential
  • C-reactive protein (CRP)
  • Blood glucose
  • Lumbar puncture (if infant stable and high suspicion)
  • Chest X-ray (if respiratory symptoms)
  • Consider: Blood gas, electrolytes, lactate

⚠️ DO NOT DELAY ANTIBIOTICS WAITING FOR LP

↓

Step 5: Empiric Antibiotic Therapy for EOS

STANDARD EOS EMPIRIC THERAPY:

AMPICILLIN

  • Dose: 50 mg/kg IV
  • < 7 days old: Every 12 hours
  • ≥ 7 days old: Every 8 hours

PLUS

GENTAMICIN

  • Dose: 4-5 mg/kg IV once daily
  • Check levels after 3rd dose
  • Target peak: 5-10 mcg/mL
  • Target trough: < 2 mcg/mL

ALTERNATIVE IF MENINGITIS SUSPECTED:

AMPICILLIN (same dosing as above)

PLUS

CEFOTAXIME 50 mg/kg IV

  • < 7 days old: Every 12 hours
  • ≥ 7 days old: Every 8 hours

Note: Cefotaxime preferred over gentamicin for meningitis due to better CSF penetration

Step 1: Assess Risk Factors

LOS RISK FACTORS:

  • Prematurity/Very Low Birth Weight (VLBW)
  • Central venous lines
  • Mechanical ventilation
  • Prolonged hospitalization
  • Previous antibiotic exposure
  • Parenteral nutrition
  • Invasive procedures
  • Immunocompromised state
